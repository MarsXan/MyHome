The brief

StyleWarm minimal Japandi: Japanese restraint with Scandinavian warmth. Low furniture, natural materials, few objects, a place for everything.
HouseholdA couple. Overnight guests sleep on one of the two 3-seat sofa beds.
RoomsLiving room with open kitchen and dining, master bedroom, home office, shower room off the master bedroom, WC off the hall, entrance hall, balcony.
MoodCalm. In practice: warm whites, light oak and sage; 2700 K light in the evening; oak, linen and stoneware as the main materials.
Must haveThe 8-seat sofa set you already own: two 3-seat sofa beds and two armchairs. In the kitchen: a side-by-side fridge in a frame cover, a cooker hood, a built-in dishwasher, the washing machine, and the water heater in a wall cabinet with a plain door. No sofa in the small room.
LikesLight oak, linen and bouclé, paper lanterns, plants, hidden storage, matte black details.
Hard nosGloss and chrome, busy patterns, cool-white light above 3000 K in living spaces, clutter on open surfaces.

Reading the plan

  • A clear day and night split. Both bedrooms sit together on one side; living, kitchen and entrance on the other.
  • One long social space, 8.3 m from the bedroom wall to the kitchen windows, with room to sit, eat and cook without walls.
  • Private shower, separate WC. The shower opens only from the master bedroom, so guests use the WC by the front door. That works well for a couple.
  • The living area has no window of its own. Daylight arrives from the two kitchen windows, 3 to 8 m away. It will be the darkest part of the flat, so the palette stays light, nothing tall stands between the sofa and those windows, and the lighting is layered.
  • The route from the front door to both bedrooms crosses the living room along the wall of the shower block. The layout keeps it open: 93 cm behind the sofa.
  • The kitchen is compact, 2.5 m across, and has only one wall without windows. That north wall takes the hob, its hood and the wall cabinets; the framed fridge and the water heater's cabinet go on the recess wall facing it.

Change while it's cheap

  • Rehang the front door on its other side. Hinged as drawn, the open door stands across the 150 cm hall and leaves only 55 cm to walk past. Hinged on the opposite jamb, it opens flat against the hall's end wall and the way in stays clear.
  • Put power and a data point behind the TV, on the living room's long north wall, with a conduit so no cables show.
  • Two ceiling points: one 1.45 m out from the TV wall and 1.5 m from the bedroom wall, over the coffee table; one over the centre of the dining table.
  • Switched sockets for the sideboard lamp and both bedside lamps.
  • Plan the cooker-hood duct out through the north kitchen wall before the wall cabinets go up.
  • Water and drain points beside the sink for the dishwasher and the washing machine, and a power point plus a water line behind the side-by-side fridge for its ice and water dispenser.
  • Water heater: it now hides behind a plain cabinet door, so ask the installer to confirm the cabinet gives the heater the clearances and ventilation its manual requires. Some heaters need an open base or a vent; the flue must lead outside.
  • Waterproof the shower corner to 2.1 m and fit a linear drain along its back wall; that is what allows a walk-in screen without a door.

Materials

WoodOne light oak everywhere: floors, tables, shelves, bed, the sliding wardrobe fronts and the slatted cabinet fronts. The walnut front door is the only other wood, and it reads as part of the building.
MetalMatte black for everything you touch or see up close: lamp stems, handles, taps, the shower profile, rails and frames. No chrome, and no brass mixed in. The side-by-side fridge, the dishwasher and the cooker hood are satin steel, the one exception, and they stay together in the kitchen.
Stone and tileWarm off-white 60 × 60 cm porcelain in the kitchen and hall; handmade-look 10 × 10 cm zellige behind the hob; soft sage-grey tile in the shower room and WC; off-white quartz worktops.
TextilesLinen for the curtains, bedding and sofa; bouclé for cushions; wool rugs; paper lanterns; cotton waffle towels.
The rule people break mostMixing wood tones that are nearly the same. Pick one oak and stay within a shade of it; get contrast from black and linen, never from a second, slightly different wood.

Room by room

Living room · 24.7 m²

Your 8-seat set, arranged in a U that faces the TV on the long north wall.

  • 3-seat sofa bed facing the TV, its back to the walkway; the second 3-seat sofa bed on the bedroom side facing in, 70 cm off the wall so the way to the office door stays open.
  • The two armchairs close the U on the kitchen side.
  • Sofas assumed 210 × 90 cm, opening into a bed that reaches 140 cm from the back, so the coffee table moves first. Armchairs assumed 85 × 85 cm.
  • Oval oak coffee table 110 × 60 cm, 45 cm from every seat; wool rug 220 × 230 cm under the front of all four pieces.
  • Low slatted oak TV console 180 × 40 × 45 cm; 65-inch TV, bottom edge at 68 cm.
  • A round oak side table with a lamp in the corner between the sofas; paper lantern Ø60 cm over the coffee table; an olive tree beside the TV.

Dining for four

Between the armchairs and the kitchen, so it zones the open space without a wall (prompt 21).

  • Oak table 140 × 80 × 75 cm, long side parallel to the kitchen.
  • Four oak chairs with woven seats, two on each long side.
  • Rice-paper cone pendant Ø45 cm, 75 cm above the table top.

Kitchen · 9.6 m²

A U: hob on the north wall, sink under the windows, fridge and water heater on the recess wall.

  • North wall, 190 cm: drawers, the hob over pan drawers, and a cupboard. Above the hob a satin-steel chimney hood, bottom 65 cm over the hob, with a wall cabinet on each side.
  • Under the windows, 385 cm: a corner filler, a narrow pull-out, the washing machine, the built-in dishwasher, the sink under the second window, another pull-out, and the corner where it meets the recess wall.
  • Recess wall: the side-by-side fridge 90 × 72 × 178 cm in an oak frame cover, meaning side panels and a bridge cabinet over it, flush with the wall cabinets at 215 cm. Next to it a base cabinet with worktop, and above that the water heater in a wall cabinet with a plain door.
  • Worktop at 90 cm, level with the window sills; zellige behind the worktops; an LED strip under the wall cabinets.

Master bedroom · 13.3 m²

  • Queen bed 160 × 200 cm (frame 170 × 210) with an upholstered linen headboard on the south wall.
  • Oak bedside tables 45 × 40 × 50 cm with ceramic lamps.
  • Sliding-door wardrobe 240 × 60 × 220 cm opposite the bed. Sliding doors because only 75 cm remain in front of it.
  • Linen curtains on a black rod across the window; sand flatweave rug 240 × 170 cm under the foot of the bed.

Home office · 10.6 m²

No sofa: guests have the sofa bed, so this room can be a proper office (prompt 19).

  • Oak desk 140 × 70 cm on black legs against the north wall, with the window to the left: side light, no glare behind the screen.
  • Open oak bookcase 170 × 35 × 190 cm on the free stretch of the door wall.
  • Slatted oak sideboard 120 × 40 × 60 cm on the south wall for files and the printer, with a framed print above.
  • Sand rug 190 × 135 cm under the chair; linen roller blind; an 80 cm path to the balcony door.

Shower room, WC and hall

  • Shower room: walk-in shower 90 × 140 cm behind an 80 cm fixed glass screen (60 cm entry), black rain head, floating oak vanity 80 cm with a vessel basin and a lit mirror, towel rail.
  • WC: wall-hung pan with a boxed-in cistern, small floating vanity 50 cm with a round mirror.
  • Hall: slatted oak shoe cabinet 100 × 30 × 90 cm, round mirror Ø60 cm above, a hook rail on the end wall, a coir mat inside the door.

Balcony · 0.9 × 1.2 m

Treat it as a tiny garden room. The door opens outward onto it, so the two planters sit in the corners outside the door's swing. A fold-down wall shelf holds a coffee cup without taking floor space; add a warm 2700 K wall light for evenings.

Your plan doesn't show which way the flat faces, so there are two planting lists:

  • Four or more hours of sun: rosemary, thyme and lavender in one planter (they take wind well at the fourth floor), red geraniums (Pelargonium) in the other.
  • Mostly shade: English ivy (Hedera helix) trailing over the parapet, with ferns and Heuchera for leaf colour.
